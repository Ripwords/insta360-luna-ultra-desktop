## Viewfinder and HUD

The live viewfinder shows a HUD with recording time, storage remaining,
resolution, and battery. Zoom runs 1×–15× (1×–12× on firmware older than 1.1.8), and there are six capture modes —
Video, Pure, Slow-mo, Photo, Pano, and Timelapse. Photo and video capture are
both one tap.

To pan and tilt the camera, **drag anywhere on the picture**. A small stick
appears where you pressed, and the further you drag from that point the faster
the camera turns; diagonals work. Let go and it stops. A click that doesn't
move does nothing, and drags that start on a control (the pro bar, the zoom
dial, the buttons) belong to that control. The arrow keys do the same, with
Shift for full speed.

The gimbal buttons run down the left edge of the viewfinder, across from the
zoom dial: the crosshair recenters the gimbal and the camera button flips
between front and selfie. The camera only takes gimbal commands while its
preview is running, so they appear with the picture.

On a Mac, the **headphones button** below them steers the gimbal with
AirPods head tracking (AirPods Pro, AirPods Max, AirPods 3rd and 4th
generation). Turning it on recenters the gimbal and takes where you are looking
as straight ahead; after that the camera points where your head does. The
crosshair re-centres both, and dragging on the picture or an arrow key hands control
back. If nothing happens, wear both AirPods and pick a microphone other than
the AirPods: as a microphone they switch to a hands-free mode that sends no
motion. macOS asks once for Motion & Fitness access.

The Camera page also has a **Webcam mode** switch, which sends the live preview
to OBS so the camera can act as a webcam. See
[Use as a webcam (OBS)](/docs/webcam).

## Pro bar

An always-visible strip over the viewfinder, grouped into three chips:

- **Exposure** — ISO, shutter, EV, and white balance.
- **Look** — colour mode, the Leica and cinematic filters, and filter strength.
- **Format** — resolution, framerate, and aspect ratio.

Scrolling over the picture zooms, and so does dragging the dial on the right
edge. While a picker is open, scrolling over its list scrolls the list instead.

## Gallery

A date-grouped grid with photo/video filtering and three thumbnail sizes.
Opening an item shows a full-screen preview with metadata and keyboard
navigation.

## Multi-select

Click a thumbnail to toggle it, shift-click for a range, or select a whole day
at once. Select-all covers the current filter. Selecting anything brings up a
floating action bar for downloading or deleting the selection.

## Downloads

Downloads run as a background queue with per-file progress. Videos stream
straight from the camera to your Downloads folder. While one is in progress it
appears there as `<name>.part`, and it gets its real name only once it is
complete. When the camera doesn't report a file's size, the bar has no
percentage and the row shows the bytes transferred so far instead.

If the camera stops sending data for 30 seconds, for example because it went
out of Wi-Fi range, the download fails with "The camera stopped sending data".
Select **Retry** on the row to start it again. If the retry stalls the same
way, turn the camera off and on again, then retry.

While a download is receiving data, the app doesn't treat slow camera replies
as a lost connection, because a camera busy sending a large file answers
slowly. If the camera really is gone, the app shows it as disconnected within
about two and a half minutes of the last data received.

## Watermark

Photos are watermarked on download with the genuine Insta360 Luna Ultra asset,
placed according to the camera's real aspect-ratio layout table. Video
watermarking is not implemented yet — videos transfer untouched.

## Delete

Deleting removes files from the camera's own storage over the control channel.
This is permanent, and the app asks for confirmation first.

## 3D showpiece

The camera is rendered from its own hi-fi 3D scan, with orbit controls, in
black or white to match the current theme.

## Themes

Two colourways are available: Arctic (light) and Midnight (dark).
